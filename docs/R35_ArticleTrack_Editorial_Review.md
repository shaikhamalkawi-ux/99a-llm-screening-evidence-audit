# R35 independent first-reader scientific and venue-fit assessment

**Date:** 10 November 2026. **Status:** Scientific development, **not final journal acceptance or submission approval**.

## Scientific judgment

The R35 extension analyzes a real sensitivity gap in the simulated *human-in-the-loop* (HITL) claims discussed in the R34 evidence audit: the source's ideal correction changes false positives but assumes no reviewer introduces new false negatives. Using only Janoudi's fixed source confusion matrix and two published routing policies, R35 enumerates a sharp conditional count envelope with two possible types of human override. The key original mathematical demonstration is that an erroneous human rejection can decrease recall **while precision remains above the machine-only baseline**, so a simulated precision improvement does not guarantee preserved evidence retention. The 80%/85% correction thresholds are numerical properties of the published counts, not claimed observed performance. This is a defensible contribution in research evaluation practice, not a generic first-invention claim.

No new randomized or prospective validation is implied. The 16,364 count states are the finite space of simple deterministic decisions, not repeated experiments. Human review-error rates, independent reference reliability, minutes, salaries, clinical consequences, and global publication prevalence remain unknown. This post-hoc extension cannot estimate their distributions.

## Article-type decision

R34's *Review* type is at risk if interpreted as a comprehensive systematic/scoping review, because the inherited 60-source pool lacks a complete database export, independent eligibility flow and PRISMA records. R35 produces an **Article-track, original source-linked methodological reanalysis** derivative in the MDPI Publications LaTeX class. The body still includes a bounded documentary context with 54 source-checked reports and an 11-work discovery register, while its principal claim is newly executed, source-constrained algebra. No editorial office has pre-approved this type, and the original R34 Review baseline remains intact.

## Remaining gates for a submission-ready Article

The original five reports were chosen *after* access and are not a random sample of the field. A methodological Article can legitimately analyze a few cases but must state the selection basis and not generalize frequencies. Reviewers could still consider five post-hoc cases and elementary algebra insufficiently generalizable. The manuscript should position the new inference as an **application**, not a universal framework. Author identities/affiliations/contributions, conflicts, funding, final AI assistance disclosure, source-rights policy and any article type/venue decision require author approval before submission.

The strongest justified deliverable is a reproducible source-view counterexample and falsification-sensitive reading rule, not a claim that AI screening harms reviewers, that humans necessarily err, or that automated literature review is ineffective.
