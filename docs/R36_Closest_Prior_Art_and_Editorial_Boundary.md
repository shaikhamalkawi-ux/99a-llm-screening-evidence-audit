# R36: Independent novelty and editorial boundary

**Date:** 10 November 2026. **Scope:** external source-identity and abstract/method visibility only where indicated. No addition to the R35 60/54/21/11 scientific evidence layers.

1. Adam et al. (2026), *Artificial intelligence tools for evidence synthesis in the biomedical field: A living rapid review and evidence map*, *Research Synthesis Methods*, DOI https://doi.org/10.1017/rsm.2026.10118 (online 8 October 2026). Publisher/PubMed abstract: 134 selected AI evidence-synthesis tool studies. It has a broader AI/ES task scope and a documented database workflow. This further disallows claiming novelty for an exhaustive evidence map or estimating prevalence from the project's 54 historical source-note records.
2. Descamps et al. (2026), *Artificial intelligence in systematic reviews and meta-analyses: Task-specific performance, residual error quantification, and human oversight*, *Orthopaedics & Traumatology: Surgery & Research* 112(6):104793, DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.otsr.2026.104793 (online 16 July 2026, issue October 2026). Public article describes residual uncertainty after manual checks using existing Clopper–Pearson/rule-of-three methods. It is adjacent, not a demonstration of R36's specific fixed-source two-error-channel count geometry, and it limits first-to-model-reviewer-error novelty language.
3. Nunnery (2026), DOI https://doi.org/10.1017/rsm.2026.10116, already develops workflow-induced evaluation targets; Lai et al. (2026), DOI https://doi.org/10.1017/rsm.2026.10114, develops verification-adjusted workload accounting. Both precede R36; no claim that our source certificate invents either idea.

**Contribution boundary:** exact, runnable, publisher-source-conditional checks (five cases) plus a joint precision–recall count feasibility demonstration for one paper's two simulated queues. It is arithmetic under specified assumptions, not a new machine-learning algorithm, risk score, empirical trial, systematic search, general theory, staff-time benefit, or human-error estimate.

**Journal-type gate:** MDPI's Article type expects substantial original research. A Review expects comprehensive critical literature synthesis and does not permit presenting new unpublished data as if it were a systematic synthesis; neither label itself repairs the inherited non-systematic eligibility pathway. The general MDPI menu also includes Technical Note/Brief Report, but availability and suitability for *Publications* require editor decision. Source: https://www.mdpi.com/about/article_types and https://www.mdpi.com/journal/publications/about. Until author identities, contributions, affiliations, funding, conflict declarations, repository permissions/license, and article-type choice are approved, R36 is **pre-submission**, with **Major Revision/editorial consultation** appropriate.
