## Methods: independent record-level reanalysis of an observed human-screening source

We conducted an independent post-hoc source reanalysis of the versioned Vallamchetla et al. (2025) reviewer-decision dataset (Mendeley Data DOI 10.17632/4bgt2p5hrd.1). Its 1,003 candidate records each provide one original-study reference decision and four separate human reviewer decisions: A1/B1 used LLM-generated PICOS summaries, whereas A2/B2 screened without those summaries. We recovered the original CSV from the public source DOI, locked its SHA-256 digest, and reproduced the reviewer-by-reference confusion matrices. We then enumerated errors and their overlaps at the record level. As a separate, explicitly counterfactual analysis, we replayed an Include-only review rule by retaining an A1-positive record only when the already observed independent A2 or B2 label also said Include. The source reviewers did not actually execute this sequential workflow, and none of the four reviewer-total time observations was repurposed as time for the replay. We did not rerun the LLM, independently re-adjudicate inclusion truth, claim randomized allocation, or infer a causal effect of assistance.

## Results: observed human disagreement and a conditional override witness

All 1,003 reference records and the four reviewer columns had complete valid binary decisions. Of the 25 reference-positive citations, A1 and B1 each retained 25, whereas A2 retained 22 and B2 retained 23; their false-negative sets were disjoint. For the 978 reference-negative citations, A1 and B1 each had one false positive, and those were also disjoint. Overall, 17 individual reviewer-to-reference errors occurred on 15 distinct records. The reconstructed (TP, FP, FN, TN) counts were A1=(25,1,0,977), A2=(22,7,3,971), B1=(25,1,0,977), and B2=(23,3,2,975), reproducing the published performance summaries. In a post-hoc Include-only replay based on the independent labels, overriding A1's 26 initially included records with A2's decisions changes the final matrix to (22,0,3,978): precision rises from 96.15% to 100%, while recall declines from 100% to 88%. Overriding instead with B2's decisions gives (23,0,2,978), 100% precision, and 92% recall. These are deterministic counterfactual outcomes from *observed* decisions, not observed follow-up adjudications or estimates of human correction rates. They provide one external record-level feasibility witness consistent with the separate Janoudi sensitivity argument.

## Discussion and limitations

The new analysis separates three evidence levels: reviewer choices that were actually recorded; error overlaps calculated from those choices; and hypothetical combinations of the choices under declared routing rules. It reinforces the need to evaluate precision jointly with retained reference-positive evidence but does not demonstrate that a two-stage human workflow would behave this way prospectively. The 25 positives came from one review topic with a reference adopted from the source authors. Reviewer identity, experience and intervention are not experimentally isolated, and the available screening times are reviewer-wide aggregate durations rather than measured replay effort. Even post-hoc perfect performance of a two-reviewer intersection against this single source reference is not evidence of general deployment safety, external validity, or net labor savings. The 60/54/21/11 documentary evidence-accounting layers remain unchanged.
