# R42 -- Editor-first comparison with closest prior work (12 November 2026)

## Why this is a focused editorial change
The early Table 1 compares six source-supported groups of prior work with numerical checks actually performed in 99a. **Prior-study scope is not the same as our denominator**. The published synthesis counts 174 (Barragan et al.) and 134 (Adam et al.) are reported as their own study totals; our 54/60 figure is a documentary source-access count, the 21 are overlapping purposive close readings, and the 11 are discovery metadata.

## Numerical examples in the reader-facing table
- Nunnery (2026) and Lai et al. (2026) already develop workflow-dependent target estimands and verification-adjusted workload: no first-invention claim. Only one of 49 frozen historical source notes documented a matched assisted-versus-unassisted human task timing contrast; absence in source notes is not absence in articles.
- Janoudi et al. (2025): 3,796 citations, 145 and 221 simulated Include-review queues. Under the original fixed binary-reference model and the stated one-decimal rounding rule, printed precisions of 81.4% and 89.9% are each compatible with only the perfect-correction state, (x,y)=(102,0) and (135,0). Does NOT imply observed human correction.
- Li et al. (2026): 6,331-record study with 113 reference positives. Under a single, common unweighted cohort assumption, displayed precision 0.34, recall 0.77 and NPV 1.00 yield seven compatible count matrices, each with 26 false negatives. Feasible mathematical reconstruction is NOT a recovered actual matrix or accusation of author error.
- Vallamchetla et al. (2025): 1,003 citations screened independently by four reviewers (published whole-task times 116/90 assisted and 463/370 unassisted minutes). Our original record-level reanalysis reveals 17 reviewer-reference discordances on 15 unique records. A post-hoc, unexecuted A1-then-A2 Include-only intersection raises precision from 96.15% to 100% while reducing recall from 100% to 88%; not an observed sequential workflow and no causal effect of aid.
- Madeyski et al. (2026) already call for Lost Evidence and meaningful non-LLM baselines. Our separate retrospective benchmark compared B0/B1 with matched 90% routing budgets on 8 SYNERGY and 22 Chan reviews: automatic final-reference positive exclusions 0/2 out of 315, and 4/6 out of 598. No fuzzy B2 measured and no human person-minutes inferred.
